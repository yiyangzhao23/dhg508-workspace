# Source notes

## What the evidence shows

The literal question — "when did Peking University first admit women?" — has
two different answers depending on what "admit" means, and the primary
documents show both:

- **February–March 1920: admitted as auditors (旁聽生).**
  Wang Lan (王蘭) entered in mid-February 1920; Xi Zhen (奚浈) and Zha Xiaoyuan
  (查曉園) followed, and by mid-March six more had joined, for nine in all.
  The university's own notice of 11 March 1920 prints them as 旁聽生 and gives
  「到校年月 = 九年二月」 (Feb 1920). They were not yet formally enrolled
  degree students.
- **Summer/Autumn 1920: formally enrolled.** After the summer entrance
  examination women were admitted as regular (正科) students.
  Cai Yuanpei, the president, put it plainly in 1934: "九年，有女學生要求進校，
  以考期已過，姑錄為旁聽生。及暑假招考，就正式招收女生。"

So the defensible answer is: **Peking University opened to women in early
1920; the first cohort (9 women) entered as auditors from February 1920 and
were formally admitted as matriculated students from the summer of 1920.** If
one insists on full, formal enrolment, the date is the summer of 1920; the
February 1920 date covers admission to classes.

## Why the sources are reliable

- The 11 March 1920 《北京大學日刊》 is a **primary, contemporaneous
  institutional document** issued by the university itself; it is not a later
  summary. It names the category (旁聽生) and the intake month.
- Cai Yuanpei's 〈我在北京大學的經歷〉 is a **first-person account by the
  decision-maker**, written in 1934 but describing events he directed.
- The 1919 晨報/民國日報 letters are **contemporaneous newspaper printings**
  of the participants' own petition, and the March 1920 民國日報 report is a
  contemporaneous press report of the first enrolments.

Note the difference between *attending classes* (旁聽) and *being enrolled*
(正科生). The course prompt's example question is exactly this ambiguity, and
the documents resolve it: admission came in two stages.

## Caveats / open questions

- In the scanned 日刊 page the 姓名 column is cut off at the bound edge, so the
  names cannot be recovered from this particular scan (see the OCR notes). The
  nine names are documented in the secondary literature and the previous/next
  issues.
- Exact ages in the table are hard to read; a secondary source gives a range of
  19–28, mean ≈22, which is consistent with the readable digits.
- Whether "开女禁" should be dated to Cai's public statement (Jan 1920),
  Wang Lan's arrival (Feb 1920), or the formal recruitment (summer 1920) is a
  genuine historiographical choice, not a factual dispute.
