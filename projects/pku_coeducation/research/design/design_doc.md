## Background and Context

- Peking University (北京大學), successor to the Imperial University of Peking
  (京師大學堂, founded 1898), was the leading national university of Republican
  China.
- After Cai Yuanpei (蔡元培) became president in January 1917 it became the
  centre of the New Culture Movement. From 1919 there was a campaign to open
  the universities to women ("大學開女禁").

## Research Questions

- When did Peking University first admit women?
- What did "admit" mean in practice: attending classes, or formal enrolment?

## Scope

- The decision and first intake, Nov 1919 – autumn 1920. Not the wider national
  spread of coeducation after 1920.

## Methodology

- Read the university's own contemporaneous notices (《北京大學日刊》) as the
  primary evidence.
- OCR the scanned notice and check it against the original image.

## Working Hypotheses

- Women were first admitted as auditors (旁聽生) in Feb–Mar 1920, then formally
  enrolled from the summer of 1920.
